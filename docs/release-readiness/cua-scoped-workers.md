# Scoped worker service evidence

Owner: Perry computer-use runtime. Scope: CuaWorkerService and its controlled subprocess fixtures. Production caller migration compiles in unsigned Apple Silicon Debug and its Cua tests pass, but lifecycle review remains open. This evidence does not establish independent production background agents or complete M1–M3.

The service reserves capacity and initial resource claims before invoking an operation. It allows three background allocations and one allocation for each auxiliary scope. Application and integration identities are case-normalized; overlapping workspace paths conflict. Dynamic claims extend an allocation without releasing its previous resources.

Each allocation owns a concrete CuaDriverClient that cannot implicitly restart after losing its connection. A handle contains private allocation identity rather than an exposed wire session or process. Capture resolves an exact target, claims its application, then requests pixels. Resolved targets have a file-private initializer and session identity; mismatched sessions and stale connection generations fail before window-state requests.

Retirement closes the handle, cancels the operation, and starts awaited process retirement. A separate completion task waits for operation acknowledgement before releasing claims. It remains independent of caller cancellation. If the operation misses the stop deadline, the allocation stays retained. Maintenance closes admission before stopping workers and runs its closure only after their completion. A failed stop leaves admission blocked; an explicit maintenance retry can succeed after the operation later finishes. A worker cannot invoke maintenance on its own service and wait for itself.

The service fixtures also cover conflicting initial and dynamic claims, cancellation during setup, stale handles, maintenance admission, self-join rejection, an uncooperative operation, and retry after delayed completion. Client tests show that resolution requests no window state and that stale or mismatched resolved targets cannot capture.

A second process fixture proves that conflicting application claims reject both capture and insertion after target resolution but before get_window_state or type_text. Shutdown waits for an already-entered maintenance closure, and that closure cannot directly await its own shutdown. If shutdown wins during the worker-stop phase, the maintenance closure cannot start. Cancellation is checked again before accepting an operation's successful return. These tests do not prove live application actions, provider execution, target closure and movement, device performance budgets, or clean-checkout reproduction. Production migration must cover foreground, background, guide, dictation, health, reload, shutdown, and erasure together with runtime and integration resource enforcement. The timeout client fixture proves rejection of later actions; confirmed process retirement remains the service owner's responsibility. Live dictation insertion, action postconditions, and independent clean-checkout review remain required. Lifecycle calls through Task.detached are not supported because they discard the task-local self-join context. No feature status is promoted by this evidence.
