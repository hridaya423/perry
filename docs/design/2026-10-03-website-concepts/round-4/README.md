# Perry: an encounter in the hero

Fourth exploration, 3 October 2026. Two images generated with built-in imagegen and visually inspected. Full prompts are in `prompts.json`.

The user rejected round three as generic despite clearer product demonstrations. Reject the headline-plus-screenshot structure. Preserve the liked living-ink identity, and build the hero around a visitor handing a cursor to Perry. Two visual treatments explore scale and figure-ground inversion, and an ink shortcut through lettering.

These are proposed website simulations. They do not capture or operate a visitor's real computer. The initial interaction should lead directly to a bounded screen-help demonstration, with acquisition available before and after it.

## Pass it here

![Pass it here](01-pass-it-here.png)

A monumental ink pointer interlocks with figure-ground lettering. The mascot reaches toward a small visitor cursor. The action is Hand it to Perry; the persistent product promise is An extra pair of hands for your Mac.

Proposed encounter:

1. Perry notices the visitor's pointer and turns toward it. The main image and acquisition link are already visible, without an intro loader.
2. Clicking Hand it to Perry starts a contained demonstration. A simulated cursor joins Perry; the visitor's real pointer remains available.
3. The monumental cursor recedes and its ink outline becomes a demo boundary. Perry guides one real click within that simulation, then offers to complete the remaining step.
4. The visitor can finish it themselves, let Perry finish, or exit. The completed result remains visible beside Get Perry for Mac.

The transition must carry spatial continuity: same character, same ink, same action location. Do not cut to an unrelated embedded video. On touch, tap the character or button. With reduced motion, crossfade to the same demo state. No cursor locking or involuntary OS input changes.

The render establishes composition and character acting; it does not demonstrate this sequence or conversion performance. The arrow overlaps the lettering strongly, so legibility during movement needs a storyboard check. A production dark mode would invert stock and ink while preserving the coral action and character visibility.

## Take the shortcut

![Take the shortcut](02-take-the-shortcut.png)

The wording becomes a winding landscape. Perry carries a pointer across one direct coral path, passing through the lettering. Clicking Take the shortcut would follow that route into the same bounded product demonstration.

This is an alternate entry composition rather than a second section to pile onto the first. The route illustrates the offer before the visitor interacts. Light mode would use dark ink and the same coral on white stock.

Visual review: both concepts avoid the screenshot-card formula and retain readable acquisition controls. The first has the clearer invitation and more expressive mascot. The second has stronger full-frame calligraphy, but the second e in easier is ambiguous and would need corrected final lettering. Neither static render establishes interaction quality; the next useful artifact is a short motion study of one chosen encounter.
